The constellation

Every dot is a satellite, colored by its job

Low orbits hug the planet and race around it every ~90 minutes. Navigation satellites sit in the middle ring. Weather satellites park over one longitude at 36,000 km and watch a whole hemisphere.

    Altitudes above low orbit are compressed so everything fits on screen.

    Cyclone Bay of Bengal · May 2023

    Cyclone Mocha

    Mocha intensified over the Bay of Bengal and made landfall near Sittwe, Myanmar. Geostationary imagers refreshed every few minutes to track the eye, while polar satellites measured winds and rainfall from inside the storm.

    What the classifier sees: a tight spiral of cloud bands around a clear eye.

    Earthquake Türkiye–Syria · Feb 2023

    Kahramanmaraş earthquakes

    A magnitude 7.8 quake ruptured hundreds of kilometres of the East Anatolian Fault. Radar satellites compared before-and-after passes (InSAR) to map exactly how far the ground moved, and sub-metre optical imagery located collapsed buildings.

    What the classifier sees: rubble textures and broken street grids, the most visually subtle class.

    Flood Sindh, Pakistan · 2022

    Pakistan monsoon floods

    Record monsoon rain turned the lower Indus valley into an inland lake. Clouds hid the ground for weeks, but radar cuts straight through them: calm floodwater reflects the pulse away and shows up black.

    What the classifier sees: smooth brown water spreading over fields and roads.

    Wildfire Maui, Hawaiʻi · Aug 2023

    Lahaina fire

    Hurricane-driven winds pushed a fast fire through Lahaina. Thermal sensors flagged hot spots from orbit, GOES-West tracked the smoke plume every few minutes, and high-res imagery mapped the burn scar afterwards.

    What the classifier sees: flame fronts, smoke plumes and charred ground.
