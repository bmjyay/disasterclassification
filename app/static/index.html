01 Cyclone · Bay of Bengal

Geostationary weather satellites image the Bay of Bengal every ten minutes. Spiralling cloud bands around a clear eye are the signature the model learns to recognise.

17.5°N 88.5°E · Meteosat / INSAT

02 Earthquake · Kahramanmaraş

After an earthquake, damage hides in texture: collapsed roofs and rubble fields. It is the subtlest class, and the one most often confused with the others.

37.2°N 37.0°E · WorldView / Pléiades

03 Flood · Indus basin

Floodwater spreads across farmland in flat, dark sheets. Radar satellites see it through cloud, so responders can map who is cut off within hours.

26.5°N 68.0°E · Sentinel-1 SAR

04 Wildfire · Sierra Nevada

Smoke plumes, active fire fronts and blackened burn scars. Thermal sensors flag new fires; imagery like this confirms what is burning and where it is heading.

39.8°N 121.4°W · Landsat 9 / VIIRS

Bring an aerial or ground photograph. The model names the calamity, shows where it looked, and surfaces the latest reporting so you can follow the response.

About the model

How predictions are made

Architecture
ResNet-50 pretrained on ImageNet with a dropout + linear head for four classes.
Training
Five epochs on the head, then full fine-tuning at a lower learning rate.
Explainability
Grad-CAM on the final residual block highlights the regions that drove the decision.
Limits
Earthquake damage is visually subtle and is the most frequently confused class.
